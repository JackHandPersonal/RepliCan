# Arms Manufacturers

Names for the makers behind the weapons in the catalogue, in the register the game already
speaks: corporate contracts, debt, surplus, salvage, and a frontier station where the tools
double as weapons. Each has a house style so a name on a receiver tells the player something,
and a suggested mapping to the Synty packs the meshes come from. Optics and accessories get
their own makers at the end. Nothing here is wired in yet; the natural home is a `maker` field
on the catalogue entry, shown in the INFO panel after the kind.

| --- | --- | --- |

| **Meridian Public Order** | Police and station-security contracts. Stun guns, shotguns, revolvers with a badge mount. Ugly, durable, logged. | Police |
| **Bastion Civil Systems** | Security-grade rifles and launchers for facilities that can afford them. Everything has a key. | Police, Military |
| **Amazonis** | Old-line military contractor. Rifles and snipers that were expensive once and are surplus now. | Military, Worlds rifles |
| **Kessler-Rand Armaments** | Heavy weapons, launchers, flamethrowers. Sells to whoever files the paperwork. | Police Launcher, Horror Flamethrower |
| **Northlight Defense Group** | Cold-worlds specialist: weapons that work with gloves on, in the dark, at minus eighty. | Worlds, Space |

## Naming notes

- Corporate names are two words and a suffix (Systems, Dynamics, Group, Company). They sound like invoices.
- Street makers are a place or a family and a trade (Ward, Street, Foundry, & Sons). They sound like a sign over a door.
- Frontier makers are a person or a landform and a plain noun (Yard, Cooperative, Outfitting).
- The pattern guns stay numbered ("Pattern 1 of the street issue") because that is how the descriptions already read.
- Helion and PCS appear as makers because the player owes one and works for the other; a gun with their name on it is a reminder.
